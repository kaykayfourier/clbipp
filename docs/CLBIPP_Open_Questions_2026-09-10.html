CLBIPP — Open Questions

Arising from the presentation-feedback document (Changes Based on Presentation Feedback) and the follow-up meeting on the pilot run.

Prepared 10 September 2026  ·  CLBIPP build team (Aamir, Khalid, Ali)

We have read the feedback document and mapped all eleven changes onto the three apps. Most of it we can build from the document alone. The questions below are the points where the document leaves a genuine choice open, or where a decision changes what we build rather than how we build it. Each carries our recommended default — if a default is right, "yes" is a complete answer and we will proceed on it. Anything marked BLOCKING stops that piece of work until we hear back.

Sections are tagged with the priority the feedback document itself assigns. Section L and M come from the meeting, not the document.

P0A. Mandatory customer battery photos

Today photos are optional at booking. Making them mandatory is a small change; the scope of "at least one" is the open point.

A1. A request can contain several battery lines (e.g. 3 e-rickshaw packs on one line, 40 lead-acid units on another). Is one photo required per line, or one photo per request overall? Our recommendation: per line. One photo of a 40-unit lot tells the dispatcher nothing about the other line on the same request.
A2. The app has a "repeat last booking" shortcut. It deliberately does not copy the old photos, because a photo is evidence of one specific consignment. Do you agree the customer must take fresh photos every time, even on a repeat? Our recommendation: yes — fresh photos always.
A3. Should we force a live camera capture, or is choosing an existing photo from the gallery acceptable? Our recommendation: allow the gallery. Warehouse staff often photograph a lot in the morning and file the request at their desk, and a camera-only rule would push them to photograph a screen instead.
A4. Is there anything the photo must show that we should state on screen — the whole lot in one frame, the label, a visible damage close-up?

P0B. Removing the preliminary estimated offer

Today the customer sees an indicative price on the review screen before submitting, and again on the confirmation screen. That number is also stored on the request and shown to the dispatcher.

B1. Should we stop showing the estimate to the customer but keep calculating and storing it for the operations team, or stop calculating it entirely? Our recommendation: keep it, ops-only. It is useful for triaging which requests to dispatch first, and it costs nothing to retain. It would be labelled "internal estimate — not shown to the vendor" on the dispatch screen.
B2. What should the customer see in its place at the moment of submitting? Nothing at all, a broad indicative range, or a line saying the price is confirmed after physical inspection? Our recommendation: a plain sentence — no number, no range. A range is an anchor too.
B3. Existing requests already carry an estimate the customer has seen. Hide it on those retrospectively as well? Our recommendation: yes, one consistent rule.

P0C. Mandatory agent inspection photos

The agent app already supports up to 8 photos per battery line and keeps them separate from the customer's photos. They are currently optional.

C1. The document lists three shots: overall lot, identifying label/model, and visible damage. Are all three individually mandatory, or is the overall shot mandatory with the other two prompted but optional? Our recommendation: overall shot mandatory; label and damage prompted as separate slots but not blocking — a corroded pack often has no readable label, and a healthy pack has no damage to photograph. Forcing all three teaches agents to shoot the floor to get past the screen.
C2. Per battery line, or one set per pickup? Our recommendation: per line — that is where the valuation attaches.
C3. Live camera capture only for agents, or gallery allowed? Our recommendation: camera-only for agents. Unlike the customer, the agent is standing in front of the battery, and this is the evidence a price dispute would turn on.

P0D. Separating inspection from collection

This is the largest of the five P0 changes. Today the app assumes one visit: the agent arrives, assesses, offers, the customer accepts, and the agent collects on the spot. Splitting the two raises questions the document does not settle.

D1. When the agent chooses "Schedule pickup for later", who picks the date — the agent on site, the customer, or the admin afterwards? BLOCKING Our recommendation: the agent proposes a date on site with the customer standing there, and the admin can change it later from the dispatch board.
D2. Does a deferred collection stay with the same agent, or go back into the dispatch pool to be reassigned? Our recommendation: stays with the same agent by default, but appears on the dispatch board as a future job so the admin can reassign it.
D3. Does an accepted price expire? If a customer accepts on the 1st and we collect on the 20th, metal prices have moved. Is there a validity window — 7 days, 14 days — and if it lapses, who re-quotes: the same agent on a second visit, or the office? BLOCKING Our recommendation: a 7-day validity stated on the offer. Past that, the price is re-confirmed by the office before collection rather than requiring a second inspection visit.
D4. If the customer cancels between accepting the offer and the scheduled collection, is there any consequence — a charge, a record against the account, or nothing? Our recommendation: nothing beyond a recorded reason, for the pilot.
D5. Can a pickup be partially collected — three packs today, the rest next week? BLOCKING Our recommendation: not in the pilot. A partial collection splits one request across two custody chains, two manifests and potentially two certificates, and that is a much larger change than the rest of this document combined. If you need it, we should scope it separately.
D6. Is a second visit to collect paid to the agent as a separate job, or is it part of the original job's fee?
D7. Should the customer be able to request a different collection date after accepting, or does that go through a phone call to the office?

P0E. Verified battery weight

The agent app already records a measured weight separately from the customer's declared weight, and prices off the measured one. What is missing is how the weight was obtained.

E1. Confirm the list of methods: digital platform scale, manufacturer label, estimated / unable to weigh. Is a weighbridge reading a fourth case worth having?
E2. When the method is "estimated / unable to weigh", should that pickup be flagged for review before an offer can be made, or is an estimated weight an acceptable basis for a price? BLOCKING Our recommendation: allow it, but flag the pickup for the admin and mark it visibly on the offer. Blocking would strand an agent at a site with a 400 kg pallet and a 50 kg scale.
E3. Should the photo of the battery on the scale be mandatory whenever the digital-scale method is used, or only above a weight or value threshold? Our recommendation: mandatory whenever the digital-scale method is chosen. It is one extra tap and it is the only thing that makes the recorded number verifiable.
E4. Will the pilot agents actually be issued scales, and of what capacity? This determines how common "unable to weigh" will be, and whether we should design around it or treat it as an exception.
E5. If the measured weight differs sharply from the customer's declared weight, should that raise a flag automatically? What tolerance would you consider material — 10%, 20%? Our recommendation: flag at 20% or 5 kg, whichever is larger, visible to the admin but not blocking the agent.

P1F. Dispatch filtering and sorting

F1. What is a "location or service area" for filtering purposes — pincode, city, or a named zone list? We already hold a zone against each agent; is there a canonical list of zones, or should we derive filters from the address pincode? BLOCKING Our recommendation: filter by city and pincode from the address, plus by the agent's zone. If you have a real zone map, send it and we will use that instead.
F2. The dispatch board currently shows only unassigned new requests. Should it become a board over the whole live pipeline — assigned, in progress, awaiting a scheduled collection — with the new requests as the default view? Our recommendation: yes, one board with filters, defaulting to what needs action.
F3. The document mentions "operational priority". Is that a value someone sets by hand, or should we derive it — waiting time, declared value, hazardous condition flags? Our recommendation: derive it for the pilot (age of request + any damage/leak flag) rather than adding a field somebody has to remember to set.

P1G. Agent live-job count

This one is already built — the assignment screen shows each agent's live job count today. Two points to confirm rather than build.

G1. We count a job as live from the moment it is assigned until the agent hands the batteries in at a facility — which means a collected load still sitting in the agent's van counts against them. Is that the definition you want? Our recommendation: yes. An agent holding five undropped pickups is not free to take a sixth.
G2. Is there a ceiling worth enforcing — a maximum number of jobs per agent per day, or a vehicle capacity in kg — where the app should warn or refuse?

P1H. Second Life vs Recycling pathway

Our decision engine already classifies every battery into one of four outcomes — reuse, refurbish, recycle, dispose. What does not exist is the operational consequence: everything collected currently flows down the recycling route, to a CPCB-registered recycler, under a dispatch manifest.

H1. Who makes the call — the engine, the agent on site, or an admin at the facility once the battery has been tested? BLOCKING Our recommendation: the engine suggests, an admin confirms at the testing stage, with a reason recorded on any change. On site the agent has neither the equipment nor the time.
H2. Where does a second-life battery physically go? We hold a list of recyclers with CPCB registration numbers. Is there an equivalent list of refurbishment or second-life partners, with whatever registration they carry? BLOCKING
H3. Does a battery that goes to second life still produce an EPR certificate for the customer, or a different document, or none? Our certificate currently reports recovered material mass, which does not apply to a battery that was never broken down. BLOCKING
H4. Should the four internal outcomes be presented to operators and customers simply as "Second Life" and "Recycling", as the document frames them? Our recommendation: yes on every screen; keep the four internally, because the price differs between reuse and refurbish.
H5. Can the pathway change after it is set — testing reveals the pack is not reusable after all? Who may change it, and does that need a written reason? Our recommendation: yes, admin only, reason mandatory and recorded in the audit trail.

P1/P2I. Battery and pickup tagging

I1. What will physically exist in the pilot — a label printer in the van, pre-printed QR rolls, or handwritten tags? This decides everything else in this section. BLOCKING
I2. A tag per individual battery, or per lot? For 40 identical lead-acid units on one line, is that 40 tags or one? Our recommendation: per lot for the pilot, per battery only for high-value lithium packs.
I3. Should we generate the codes (and you print sheets from the admin console), or will you buy pre-printed QR rolls that the agent scans and binds to a pickup in the app? Our recommendation: pre-printed rolls, scanned and bound. It removes the printer from the van, which is the part most likely to fail on a wet Tuesday.
I4. Does a tag need to survive as far as the recycler's process, or only until the battery is booked in at our facility?

P2J. QR-tracked transport containers

J1. Do the reusable containers exist yet, and roughly how many are in circulation?
J2. One container per collection run, or can a run fill several? Can one container span two runs across two days?
J3. We already record a "custody batch" when an agent hands a load in at a facility — a signed, timestamped, geotagged hand-over that produces a chain-of-custody PDF. Should a QR container simply be that record, or are they two separate things that need to coexist? BLOCKING Our recommendation: make the container the physical identity of the hand-over we already record, rather than adding a parallel structure.
J4. Is a container ever left with the facility part-full and returned later, or does it always empty on arrival?

P2K. Same-day nearby pickup grouping

K1. We do not reliably hold map coordinates for customer addresses — many were entered as free text. For the pilot, is grouping by pincode acceptable instead of by true road distance? BLOCKING Our recommendation: yes, pincode grouping for the pilot. True routing needs geocoding every address and a routing service, which is its own piece of work.
K2. Do you want a "collection run" recorded as a real thing — vehicle, driver, start and end time — or is grouping just a convenience for the dispatcher?
K3. Any hard limits per run — maximum pickups, maximum kilograms per vehicle?

FROM THE MEETINGL. The pilot, and the decision engine

Our understanding from the meeting is that the pilot runs with a small group of agents, that the automated pricing engine may not be in use for this phase, and that you want to keep a human step where the agent or customer speaks to the office. These questions test that understanding — please correct anything we have wrong.

L1. For the pilot, should automated pricing be off — the agent records the inspection and the price comes from the office — or on, with the agent able to override it? BLOCKING Our recommendation: off by default, switchable per pilot without a code change. The engine's price is only as good as the market data behind it, and a pilot is the wrong moment to discover that.
L2. If the agent can enter or adjust a price by hand, does that need office approval above a threshold, and what threshold?
L3. When the price comes "from the office", does that happen in the app — the agent requests a price, an admin sends one back, the agent reads it to the customer — or entirely by phone, with the app only recording the agreed number afterwards? BLOCKING Our recommendation: in the app, because it leaves a record of who quoted what and when. The phone call can still happen alongside it.
L4. Should the customer still see and accept the offer on their phone, or is acceptance verbal and recorded by the agent? Our recommendation: keep it on the customer's phone. It is the only evidence of acceptance we have, and it is already built.
L5. Even with the engine switched off, would you like it to keep running silently in the background and log what it would have priced, so that at the end of the pilot you can compare its numbers against the prices your team actually agreed? Our recommendation: yes. It costs nothing, changes no price, and it is by far the cheapest way to find out whether the engine is ready for the next phase.

FROM THE MEETINGM. Keeping a human in the loop

M1. What phone number should the apps show, and during what hours? Is it one number for customers and agents, or two?
M2. Do you want a "request a callback" button that raises a task in the admin console, or is a plain tap-to-call number enough? Our recommendation: tap-to-call for the pilot, plus a callback request on the two screens where people get stuck — after submitting a request, and after receiving an offer.
M3. Should agents have an in-app escalation to the office — unsafe battery, customer disputes the price, address wrong — or is that a phone call?
M4. Should a customer be able to file a pickup request by phone, with your team entering it into the system on their behalf? That is a different feature from everything above and we have not planned for it.

N1. When does the pilot start, and must all five P0 items be live before it does? BLOCKING
